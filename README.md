# ◆ The Corral

A terminal app where **twenty cheap 2024 Meta Llama 3.1 8B instances** (`meta-llama/llama-3.1-8b-instruct`, $0.05/$0.08 per M tokens) sit as **five tables of four**, all talking at once about **one shared seed**, refereed live by **TypeSafe Jev 1.13** — and occasionally **overhearing each other**.

Written in **Rust** (ratatui + tachyonfx + colorgrad on tokio). Jev is called straight
at OpenRouter's System One endpoint, so the judgement model is the whole product and
the only heavy dependency is the HTTP stack the app already needed.

```
cargo run                              # asks for a seed, then runs
cargo run -- -t "your seed"            # go straight to it (any text: a question, a topic, a sentence)
cargo run -- -t "..." -n 6             # six rounds then stop
cargo run -- --headless -t "..."       # no TUI: stream all five chats (scriptable)
cargo run -- --once                    # render one frame and exit (no network)
cargo run --bin snapshot -- --demo -o corral.svg   # a still, offline from the corpus
cargo run --bin snapshot -- -t "..." -n 10 -o corral.svg   # a still of a real run

cargo test                             # the offline suite (no network, no spend)
```

Keys: **1-5** open a table · **o** overview · **Tab/.** cycle · **t** new seed · **space** pause · **s** save · **f** flat · **h** help · **q** quit.

```
        │          ●───────────┼──────────▸          │       ─ the wire───────────────  3 eavesdrops, one-way ─
    ↳ the newest line to cross: Dexter at table 2 → table 1 — "…"
```

* **Every table band** carries its one-sentence summary — **the single most representative line, picked by Jev** (`summary` Choice), not generated by an LLM — plus its newest lines, each with its speaker, its discourse **move glyph** and a `—` into the prose. All five are always visible, down to 60×18.
* **The heat ribbon** is one row above the tables: five labelled bars whose length is that table's judged **heat**, painted from the dusk ramp, so the five rooms' temperature is comparable at a glance.
* **The wire** is the eavesdrop map drawn as a **lattice**: five labelled table columns, and each eavesdrop a thread leaving its source column, crossing the gaps and landing in its destination column. The newest carried line is quoted whole beneath it. When a line is caught crossing, a single bright braille dot **travels the thread** — the app's one animated element.
* **`⟪heard⟫`** marks a line that drifted in from another table, with a `│` rail down its rows. It lands **verbatim** and the hearing table's next reply must answer it.

## The look, and where it came from

The **2026 redesign** was put to Jev before a line was written — 36 judgements, every fork recorded with its probability in [`docs/ui-redesign.md`](./docs/ui-redesign.md), with the primary-source research behind it in [`docs/research/`](./docs/research). The brief was *prettier and more cutting-edge without cluttering the screen or sacrificing readability*, so the whole design is **subtractive**.

| fork | what Jev chose | p |
| --- | --- | --- |
| direction | refine the existing **ember-dusk parlour**, do not replace it | 0.94 |
| palette | **one accent** + **one reserved eavesdrop hue** + a neutral ramp; identity off colour | 0.93 |
| body text | off-white `#e8e6f2` — pure white scores Lc −107, past the halation cap | 0.88 |
| agent colour | a sigil's hue encodes its **voice family** (six hues, one meaning each) | 0.81 |
| chrome | **one-sided hairlines** — a band draws only the edge it shares | 0.60 |
| focus | by **lightness** — raise the focused band's rule; no fills, no new glyph | 0.58 |
| header | single-glyph hairline ramp, one content row reclaimed | 0.98 |
| transcript | speaker **inline** on the first text row — three rows a message become two | 0.77 |
| table separator | a bare **gutter**, no glyph at all | — |
| heat | a one-row **heat ribbon**: five comparable bars from the dusk ramp | 0.95 |
| the wire | keep the **block glyph lattice**; recolour to the reserved hue | 0.88 |
| live signal | **one** animated element: a **braille pulse** travelling the wire | 0.78 |
| dissolve | **removed** — it scrambled every glyph the reader was reading | 0.97 |
| colour depth | **three palettes** by terminal capability, plus `NO_COLOR` | 0.97 |

Two Jev *rankings* were overridden on its own requirement checks (read the distribution, not just the winner): the detail band layout and the per-agent state markers. Two whole candidate packs first landed under 0.5 — a hue that carries a *meaning* rather than an identity, and a heat signal that is *comparable across tables* — and both became decisive (0.81, 0.95) once the catalog contained the option the requirements were pointing at.

Design invariants enforced in `tests/` by rendering to a string at five sizes: nothing wraps or overruns the width; every table shows its **newest line**; every title stays whole; every gauge names **both** of its poles; the wire draws a `●…▸` thread between **two named tables**. When the frame is short the judge band drops its *prose* to a compact two-pole form before the transcript would starve, and the overview drops its gutters then its roster lines before the newest line would be lost.


## How an eavesdrop works

Between rounds Jev answers, for each table's latest line: *would this carry to a different table?* (`worth` Noul). The best candidate above the gate — **calibrated to 0.46** after measuring that real candidate lines score 0.40–0.53 — is then routed by two more judgements: *which table would overhear it*, and *how should it change that table* (changes what they discuss / adds a new argument / shifts their mood / gets a direct reply / is dismissed). The line lands in the hearing table, marked, and is re-anchored into its reply chain.

A real 10-round run: **3 eavesdrops**, 58 lines, **$0.0117**, 143 Jev calls.

## Jev is the product

Every table, every turn, is **one batched speculative fan-out** answering many judgements over the same state:

| primitive | judgements per table-turn |
| --- | --- |
| **Choice** | who speaks next · which line the reply answers · the one-line **summary** · the table's current **thread** · the line's voice family · its discourse **move** · the **figure** it asserts · the final **outcome** |
| **Noul** | the four gauges (heat / consensus / drift / novelty) · **cohesion** · does the line **assert a checkable claim** · four **stance** probabilities · three **belief-tracking** propositions · the eavesdrop **worth** |
| **Score** | **originality · clarity · insight** per line, normalized and **composited in code** (weights in `room::QUALITY_WEIGHTS`) |

And they drive the app: **confidence-gated routing** (an unsure pick explores a quiet llama), **belief tracking** (three sparklines per table), **cohesion** scored per line, and **one-way eavesdrops** decided by judgement, not a coin flip.

## Design provenance

Every fork went through Jev, including an **adversarial critique of the ask itself**. `jev_verify` confirmed all six of my understanding-claims against your literal words; `jev_classify` then sorted the *underspecified* parts, and the three that genuinely changed the build went back to you (all five tables share **one** seed; this **replaces** the old 20-in-one chat; eavesdrops are **one-way**). Jev also chose the mechanism (`judged_leak_quote`, 0.80) and the summary source (`jev_representative_line`, 0.67), and flagged the two traps: eavesdropping **breaks the reply chain** (guard, 0.98 — hence the ⟪heard⟫ re-anchor) and five tables ≈ **5× the judgement calls** (guard, 1.00 — measured, not assumed).

## Cost

A 10-round run is about **$0.012** — five tables of judging for a little over a cent.

## A note on the completion gate

`jev_gate` over the raw `src/judge.rs` diff has **escalated** on every revision: it cannot confirm completion claims and scores `correctness` confidence low, while claims are rarely *contradicted* — a known TypeSafe jaggedness on code diffs. The substantive evidence (54 passing tests, live runs, measured leak scores) is what backs the "done" claim here.
